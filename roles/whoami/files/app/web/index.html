Your other names

Every way of tracking sees a different identity. Each one below changes under different conditions, so compare them after each experiment.

    Other browsers on this device and network

    These scans came from a different browser but share your device name and IP address. That pairing is how trackers link your browsers together.

      Try to lose your name

      • Open a private window

        Private windows start without cookies, so your cookie name changes. If your browser name stays the same, sites can still recognize you there.

      • Switch browsers

        Your browser name changes, but your device name usually doesn't: it's built from your screen, time zone and hardware, which every browser on this computer reports alike.

      • Turn on a VPN

        Your network name changes and your location moves. Your browser name stays put, and your clock may now disagree with your IP address.

      • Try a privacy browser

        Tor Browser and Mullvad Browser make everyone look the same. Brave and Safari change some readings every session. Either way, a stable name is harder to pin on you.

      What gave you away

      Does your story add up?

      Bot-detection and fraud-prevention services cross-check everything a browser reveals. A mismatch can get you blocked even when your fingerprint is common.

        WebRTC leak test

        Video-call technology can reveal your real IP address even behind a VPN or proxy. The test asks a public STUN server () which address your browser comes from; nothing else is sent.

        How this works

        Fingerprinting

        The page reads about forty properties that any script can access without permission: screen, fonts, graphics card, audio stack, time zone and more. None is unique alone. Together they usually are.

        The server adds what it sees in the connection itself. This service terminates TLS on its own instead of behind the usual proxy, so it can read your browser's handshake and HTTP/2 settings the way CDNs and bot-detection services do (JA4, JA3 and Akamai's HTTP/2 format).

        Names

        Each identity is a keyed hash. The name, animal, colours, rosette and code are drawn from its bits, so the same fingerprint always gets the same name and a different one almost never does. The passport's machine-readable zone encodes your code, first visit and deletion date, with real check digits.

        FingerprintJS is the open-source library many sites embed; it runs here with its telemetry switched off.

        What's stored

        For each fingerprint: keyed hashes of its attributes, the first and last scan date, a visit count, a coarse label like "Firefox on Linux", and a keyed hash of the IP address so browsers on one network can be linked. No IP addresses, User-Agent strings or raw values are stored.

        Records are deleted {{.RetentionDays}} days after their last scan, are never backed up, and "Forget this browser" deletes yours immediately. No third party is contacted unless you run the WebRTC test.

        Location and network data: IP Geolocation by DB-IP (CC BY 4.0). Tor exits: the Tor Project's bulk exit list. Browser library: FingerprintJS (MIT). TLS fingerprints: JA4 (BSD) and JA3. Typeface: Atkinson Hyperlegible (OFL).
